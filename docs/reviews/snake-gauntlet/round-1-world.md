# Round 1 — independent world / visual critique

**Verdict: LOSE against the stated Snake Pass visual bar.** This is a known-identity comparison, not a blind test. I inspected the actual `round-1-overview.jpg` runtime capture and official Snake Pass `screenshot_0.jpg` and `screenshot_1.jpg`. Animation, character behavior, effects in motion, and other scenes are **unverified**.

## What the capture earns

The board is legible, the snake route is readable, and the framing gives spectators a useful overview. The arch, planted border, carved perimeter, and trees establish a coherent ruins theme. The interface has a considered palette and typography. These are real strengths, but the screenshot supports a presentable diorama, not the requested fully finished commercial-quality world.

## Biggest remaining gap

**The composition is dominated by a huge, mechanically repeated tile surface, while the character and environment have too little visual presence or material richness.** The leaf motif repeats across almost every square and becomes the loudest texture in the scene. The snake is a small green tube against green flooring. The reference establishes expressive character silhouettes, sculpted and weathered surfaces, layered vegetation, atmospheric depth, and stronger separation between lit and shaded forms. The grid requirement does not prevent those qualities; they can be concentrated around the board and on its hero character.

## Three high-impact changes

1. **Rebuild the visual hierarchy around the snake.** Subdue the repeating floor motif substantially, use restrained stone variation and fine grid joints, and give the character stronger value/color separation. Make the head, eyes, cheek/muzzle shape, and taper expressive and recognizable at the default overview distance. The collectible also needs a clearer silhouette and stronger focal treatment; its current pale green form almost disappears into the field.
2. **Give the island depth, craftsmanship, and directional light.** Add a few substantial, asymmetric perimeter forms—stepped ruins, rooted stonework, clustered vegetation, a coherent tree canopy—and a distant environmental layer. Keep all playable cells unobstructed. Replace uniformly pale blocks with varied warm/cool stone surfaces, softer bevels, localized wear/moss, and grounded crevice/contact shadows. The current teal surround, faceted leaf clusters, and evenly lit floor feel assembled; the reference's atmospheric planes, sculpted masses, and purposeful sun/shadow relationships feel authored.
3. **Remove visible unfinished product states.** The UI reports **“2300% of the garden occupied”**, which is plainly broken. “preserve-space: reachable=426 / foodDistance=13, tail=true” reads as exposed debugging rather than spectator information. Fix the percentage and replace those diagnostics with a short human-readable action/status. This is an immediate completeness blocker even before the art gap is addressed.

Do not interpret the reference's close-up camera as a requirement to abandon the spectator overview. Preserve the readable board while raising character prominence, surface quality, lighting, and the surrounding world's depth. No screenshot evidence supports a tie or win yet.
