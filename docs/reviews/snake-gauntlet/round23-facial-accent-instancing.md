## Smallest bounded slice and implementation

Three redundant opaque face draw objects were removed without removing facial details: two separate recessed nostril ellipsoids now share **one** two-instance mesh; each eye's two original white catchlights now share **one** two-instance mesh (two batches for the two moving eye parents). Total serpent face/body mesh objects **24 → 21**. The original six world/local ellipsoid transforms, positions, materials, gaze-parent relationships and facial expression remain intact. A shared preallocated high/low sphere geometry is switched with the same preexisting detail LOD; instances use immutable local matrices, without per-frame allocation. Instance-specific GPU buffers are explicitly disposed on renderer/character cleanup. The original two pupils, eyelids, tongue, skull, jaw, body, portal discontinuities, contact and all public gameplay cues remain.

New `tests/phase3/snake-face-accent-batching.test.cjs` checks instance count, exact six matrices to 1e-6, three batching groups, preallocated high/low geometry, 120 alternating quality/expression frames, immutable resource counts, two discontinuous body paths, independent pupils and disposal. This new test was not independently executed against the old source as a red baseline; do not claim a demonstrated red/green run. During the first full candidate CI, **550/552 Node** passed: the two failures were existing floor-contact probes that treated shared instanced **unit-sphere** geometry as if it had no per-instance transforms, resulting in false underground eye-highlight readings. The test-only `lowestHeadPoint` audit now multiplies each instance transform with the mesh's world matrix. It preserves the exact original floor thresholds, and also works for ordinary meshes. No gameplay geometry was raised or hidden to make the test pass.
