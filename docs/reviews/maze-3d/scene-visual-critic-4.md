# Scene visual critic 4 — blind actual-capture review

Review date: 2026-10-30.

## Verdict locked before identity disclosure

**A wins narrowly over B for the combined world, character, and encounter-readability brief. The fixed Captain Toad visual bar remains unpassed.** This is a visible refinement, not evidence of a substantially more developed character or a newly coherent adventure scene.

I inspected the six anonymous actual-output images first: desktop, explorer detail, and guardian encounter for A and B. I then sent the A verdict before opening the two fixed reference images. No source, prior review, or version identity informed that choice. This review uses only the supplied software-balanced captures. I did not inspect a stale full-quality capture, use a browser, or infer motion, sound, frame rate, agent correctness, or off-camera world completeness from the stills.

After the verdict was locked, the lead disclosed that **A is the fourth/current pass (round 04)** and **B is the third/prior pass (round 03)**, with original captures preserved under `evidence/maze-3d/captures`. That disclosure does not change the judgment.

## Stronger actual output against the fixed bar

The two reference images are visibly recognizable as Captain Toad. The A/B comparison was blind; the absolute reference comparison cannot be blind to Nintendo's familiar art direction. I therefore judge visible construction, composition, character staging, and surface separation rather than brand familiarity or an assumed production budget. Different camera framing and rendering conditions also limit pixel-for-pixel comparison.

### What A already establishes

A reads as an original 3D garden-temple setting rather than an abstract grid alone. The hero and guardian are separate modeled forms with useful silhouettes. Cream masonry, dark teal accents, cyan water, gold objects, and green plants belong to a coherent palette. Stairs and door arches establish elevation and passage. The interface makes the current broad goal, elapsed progress, and the existence of autonomous exploration explicit.

### Where the actual frame falls short

**The largest remaining gap is authored scene storytelling: the local terrain, character attitude, threat, and destination do not yet form one readable adventure moment.** In `A-danger.png`, the explorer faces the viewer while a guardian occupies another part of the corridor. The warning label supplies the emotional meaning. Without that label, the still reads primarily as two separately placed figures in an attractive maze.

In `toad-scene.jpg`, the visible stage gives the eye a hierarchy of tall arches, a long blue ladder, massive teal structures, elevated platforms, grouped obstacles, and bright collectibles. These have distinct sizes and spatial roles. Much of the stage boundary and its vertical organization are visible together. A has steps and arches, but repeated cream corridors, parapets, and dark vertical extrusions dominate the frame; its local landmarks do less work to organize the adventure. The cropped A views cannot establish that the entire world is incomplete, but they do not demonstrate the reference's composed whole-stage readability.

In `toad-maze.jpg`, the character's side-on orientation, forward headlamp, adjacent crossing platforms, water channel, coins, and tower entrance create a clear local situation. The character belongs to the action suggested by the environment. A's explorer is recognizable and three-dimensional, but its repeated front-facing stance and compact facial features communicate less attitude or intention in the supplied views.

The reference also separates materials more convincingly. Stone has rounded edges and local surface variation; foliage has finer silhouette variety; water has highlights, changes in tone, and visible depth information. In A, stone and plant shapes remain broad and repetitive, the teal water is comparatively uniform, and dark wall faces lose a great deal of shape. The hero's small mechanical parts and the guardian's dark carapace could use stronger form separation. These are observations about the provided actual output, not a claim about an unavailable higher-quality rendering mode.

## Next builder priorities — maximum three

1. **Stage the actual guardian encounter as a readable decision.** Use the real encounter state to orient the explorer's head, body, and lantern toward the threat or the escape direction; give the guardian a distinct anticipatory posture and readable attention direction. Frame the pair with the available junction, passage, or refuge so the viewer can identify the threat and the explorer's likely response from the scene. Keep a concise truthful intent sentence visible if the rendered action alone cannot explain the decision. Acceptance evidence: an actual encounter capture where the relationship remains understandable with the warning label covered. This asks for visible staging; the current stills provide no basis to judge animation quality.

2. **Build one distinctive navigable landmark and surrounding terrain composition.** Replace a locally repetitive stretch with a clearly designed courtyard, tower, bridge, or other landmark that has a recognizable silhouette, meaningful height relationships, and a readable entrance and onward route. Give its immediate area a small number of purposeful environmental details and planting groups. Preserve clear walkable space. Acceptance evidence: an actual overview plus approach capture where a viewer can recognize the same place and explain how its visible routes connect, without relying on the map.

3. **Finish character forms and material separation at the actual software-balanced quality.** Prioritize the explorer's face and silhouette, clean transitions between torso and limbs, distinct glove/boot/lantern shapes, and guardian eyes/front plate/leg separation. Improve local contact shadows, bevel definition, and restrained material variation so cream stone, dark shell or cloth, metal, leaves, and water remain visibly different at the delivered capture size. Acceptance evidence: actual desktop and close captures where the face, hands, carried object, and guardian attention direction read immediately. More tiny accessories alone would not close this gap.

These priorities address the remaining bar rather than treating A's narrow win as completion. The current evidence supports progress in clarity and polish, but not parity in developed character acting, landmark composition, or finished scene materials.
