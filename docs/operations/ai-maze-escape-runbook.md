# AI Maze Escape Operations Runbook

## Operating principle

Preserve authoritative truth before availability. When simulation, persistence, interaction, or public-output integrity is uncertain, activate the intentional safe scene, reject new paid-eligible influence, fence the writer where authority may diverge, preserve evidence, and resume only after verified restore and output verification. A technical incident is never classified as a fair game loss.

## Roles and authority

- **Viewer:** read-only public and operational status.
- **Operator:** safe scene, disable interactions, disable public text, restart isolated components, request a snapshot, and select a bounded quality preset.
- **Administrator / on-call lead:** verified restore, fresh run, configuration/content/deployment rollback, credential actions, and emergency halt.
- **Independent witness:** confirms production-equivalent drill conditions, observes automated and operator actions, verifies public output, and signs the evidence digest.

Every control is environment-scoped, role-gated, idempotent, and durably audited. Direct mutation of database rows, snapshots, event journals, or in-memory authority is prohibited.

## Universal incident sequence

1. Declare the incident ID, exact candidate checksum, channel/run ID, environment, and owner.
2. Protect public output with the safe scene when the current frame may be false, private, frozen, black, silent, or stale.
3. Disable new paid-eligible effects whenever moderation, entitlement, idempotency, audit, persistence, or provider truth is uncertain.
4. Fence the current writer before restore, replay, or replacement of the simulation process.
5. Preserve logs, event/command ranges, snapshots, checksums, provider envelopes, control audit, captures, alerts, and resource samples.
6. Use the smallest declared recovery action. Do not rewrite history or transform a technical result into a game result.
7. Resume only after authoritative checksum verification, public-output verification, and two healthy monitoring samples.
8. Record start/end time, automated actions, operator actions, witness, pass/fail, evidence digest, follow-up owner, and rollback decision.

## Verified restore procedure

1. Activate the safe scene and reject new paid-eligible effects.
2. Fence the active writer lease.
3. Select snapshots newest-first by exact game, deterministic, configuration, and content compatibility.
4. Reject invalid record or envelope checksums and continue only to the next compatible snapshot.
5. Replay the contiguous durable command journal in sequence.
6. Compare authoritative state, belief map, route, inventory, influence ledger, event sequence, and final checksum.
7. Acquire a newer lease generation for the replacement worker.
8. Rebuild presentation from a privacy-safe render snapshot and verify frame, scene, HUD, caption, and audio health.
9. Resume only after all checks pass; otherwise quarantine and start a clearly declared fresh run.

## Rollback and fresh-run boundary

Source, deployment, config, content, assets, provider adapters, and feature flags are one frozen compatibility set. A rollback that cannot load current snapshots/events must quarantine or complete the current run and begin a new declared run on the prior set. Historical events, results, records, and audience acknowledgements are append-only and are never rewritten.

## Evidence and sign-off

Every production-equivalent drill requires an exact candidate checksum, real timestamps, owner, independent witness, environment and infrastructure identity, runbook version, automated-action proof, operator audit, output capture, state/checksum evidence, pass status, and content-addressed evidence digest. Synthetic CI drills prove implementation only; they do not satisfy the R5 production gate.
