# Round 01 — independent visual review

Unblinded direct visual A/B inspection of all four supplied reference screenshots and all four round-01 candidate screenshots, plus supplied reference provenance manifest. Titles and scene context are recognizable. No builder claims, source code, prior review, or runtime animation inspected.

## Verdict

FAIL: current output does not win the visual comparison. The Room 4 references clearly lead room and mechanism craft; the Escape Simulator reference leads visible character finish and scene integration. Static evidence does not establish every character/environment finished or autonomous behavior.

## Largest remaining gap

The room focal assets still read as assemblies of repeated primitives with highly uniform materials. The blank door with a torus handle is the strongest example. UI polish cannot compensate for missing dimensional mechanical design, material variation, contact grounding, and authored visual storytelling.

## Room

Reference wins clearly.

- Both bookcases use repeated rectangular volumes and sparse, similarly arranged book blocks. The arch, door, trim and furniture have very simple silhouettes and mostly hard primitive joins.
- Wood is strongly orange, uniformly glossy, and visibly repeated across floor, shelves, trim and pedestals; large black ceiling and door areas lack the reference's layered material and silhouette detail.
- Reference lighting guides attention through local pools of warm light, shadowed recesses, and material-specific response. Candidate light is broader and objects show weak contact grounding in this still.

**Concrete next fix:** Build one bespoke focal zone around the door: recessed layered panel, beveled frame, inset architectural trim, hinges and bolts, aged metal lock assembly and localized warm illumination. Reduce orange wood saturation and gloss; distinguish floor, cabinet and trim grain scale/roughness. Add darker recesses and contact shadows. Give nearby shelves a small number of distinctive, purposefully composed artifacts instead of more repeated blocks.

## Character

Reference wins the visible finish comparison; candidate character is only shown in a small side view.

- Candidate robot is mainly smooth cream head/torso volumes with simple tube limbs, gold joints and a flat packed accessory silhouette. The bright cream surfaces separate strongly from the room without much fine material or construction detail.
- Reference avatar has readable shaped clothing, cuffs, hands, boots, hair and accessories, with coherent stylized surface response and scene lighting. A robot can meet this finish target without becoming human or photorealistic.

**Concrete next fix:** Refine the robot as an authored porcelain-and-brass automaton: intentional asymmetry, overlapping shell seams, recessed optic housing, engraved identity plate, visible hinge/piston construction, and a dimensional carried tool. Give porcelain, brass, leather and glass different roughness and add grounding/rim light consistent with the room. Supply a face/front three-quarter close view for a real character review.

**Evidence limit:** No claims about facial animation, gait, interaction animation, rigging, or unseen characters.

## Mechanisms and UI

Reference wins mechanisms by the largest category gap. Candidate interface is readable at desktop size but does not establish a finished tactile game experience.

- The image labelled mechanism is largely a blank dark door panel with a gold ring, a shelf and a diagram board. It exposes no comparable layered machinery, readable mechanical linkage, engraved control, gauge, or lock state.
- The reference workbench and safe use deep assemblies, varied metal treatment, fasteners, toothed parts and a clear relationship between controls and housing.
- Candidate UI repeats the title and keeps a substantial investigation sidebar visible during close inspection; the world mechanism has little visual information to compete with that text.
- Result capture shows a black exit area with a bright mint frame/handle. It simultaneously displays 8/8 locks open, 6/8 discovered, Opening the exit, and Escape confirmed 100%. This may be an intermediate capture and is not proof of a persistent state bug.
- Mobile layout fits the narrow screen but crops out the robot and much of both side zones; small in-world help/status text is hard to read and the lower investigation state is compressed into adjacent columns.

**Concrete next fix:** Replace the ring-on-panel focal mechanism with a layered 3D assembly whose visible controls match the puzzle: engraved dial, toothed moving rings, locking pins, mounting plates, screws, sockets and restrained state lights. Frame it tightly with a dedicated inspection camera and a compact contextual state readout. For the ending, show a designed revealed space/light beyond the door and a stable, coherent terminal state. For mobile, use a purpose-composed camera and one-column investigator state with comfortably readable controls.

**Evidence limit:** No interaction or animation quality inferred from stills. No stable terminal-state runtime inspection performed.
