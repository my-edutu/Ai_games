# Round 03 independent visual review

## Verdict

**A is stronger for room, mechanism, mobile, result, and overall. A still does not meet the complete, developed standard established by the fixed references.** These are separate judgments. B remains a sparse, very dark primitive scene; A is a coherent furnished room with an identifiable character and a substantial vault mechanism. The remaining gap is visible in the art itself, not just in comparison labels.

## Review protocol and limits

I inspected the eight randomized A/B PNGs first and sent the A/B verdict to the parent before opening the four fixed reference images or the separate character closeup. I did not read the alias mapping, source code, prior reviews, or builder discussions, and did not use a browser. The chronological identities remain unrevealed in this review.

The blinding is limited: the scenes, interface, lighting, and camera framing differ enough that development level is plainly visible. This is a chronology-blinded preference judgment, not a matched-scene controlled test. The reference comparison is explicitly unblinded. Static screenshots do not verify interactions, animation, collision, or runtime correctness.

### Comparison qualifications

- **Room:** A still has rigid bilateral staging, repeated book shapes, and simple furniture profiles. The character is almost entirely cut off at the right edge of this wide view.
- **Mechanism:** The cameras do not match: A isolates its vault, while B is an oblique wider view. That framing advantage must not be mistaken for the entire improvement. A has real visible part complexity beyond the framing difference.
- **Mobile:** A crops the room laterally, so the vault dominates and peripheral objects disappear. This is an acceptable crop in the supplied still, but it cannot establish navigation or access to every control.
- **Result:** A reveals a flat-looking gray barred panel or short passage beyond the open vault, so the destination has much less depth and finish than the surrounding room.

## Fixed-reference comparison — explicitly unblinded

### Environment

A is recognizably a furnished 3D escape room and is well beyond the primitive stage visible in B. It does not yet match the fixed reference bar for a completely developed environment. The library reference has deep built-in casework, varied proportions, carved profiles, dense but purposeful objects, asymmetry, and shadow/roughness variation that makes the room feel inhabited. A still reads as repeated simple furniture placed in a symmetrical set, with large uniform wood surfaces and relatively flat contact/light treatment. The stylized Egypt reference also demonstrates that this depth of authorship is compatible with stylization; photorealism is not required.

B is substantially below both the library and the stylized Egypt environment references. Dark exposure obscures form and the visible scene remains sparse, largely primitive geometry. It does not establish a developed setting.

### Mechanisms

A has a developed mechanism silhouette and a meaningful hierarchy of large and small parts. Against the workbench and safe references, it still looks shallow and diagrammatic: simple extruded parts, fairly uniform brass, and broad mottled dark metal. The reference machines have more distinct material identities, shaped housings, varied component profiles, and stronger readable depth at their connections. A is credible as an intermediate game asset, but these images do not establish parity with the fixed mechanism references.

B does not approach the fixed mechanism bar. A ring on a blank door and an obscured wheel do not supply the construction, layered part relationships, or material development visible in either mechanical reference.

## Separate actual character closeup

The separate closeup shows a deliberately designed cyclopean hovering Archivist: a large glass/lens eye with nested brass rims, antenna, cream torso shell, chest badge and vents, jointed arms, articulated fingers, brass collars, and a lower propulsion unit. Its palette and mechanical language fit the room. It is a recognizable character, not merely a sphere or unadorned capsule placeholder.

The robot is a valid developed stylized character direction. A human body, realistic face, or photorealistic skin is not required to meet the character brief. The fixed stylized avatar is relevant for coherent proportions, silhouette, authored secondary detail, and scene integration rather than for human likeness.

The character is less polished than the fixed stylized avatar in form transitions and finish: its torso, elbow bands, and finger pieces remain simple, and the hover effect is a conspicuous hard triangular cone. More consequentially, the room-wide image cuts almost the whole character off, while the result image places it high against the shelf. That presentation prevents the developed character from contributing consistently to the room.

The still does not demonstrate gesture, animation, deformation, expressiveness over time, or whether the hovering pose is stable.

## Single largest gap

**Authored environment construction and staging.** The dominant desk/chair/pedestal area and mirrored shelving still read as a small kit of simple repeated forms. The provided library and stylized Egypt scenes both use more individually shaped objects, more purposeful relationships between props, and more convincing local depth. This gap affects most of every wide and mobile screenshot.

Develop the dominant desk/chair/pedestal grouping as one convincing, individually proportioned furniture ensemble with visible construction, selective local detail, and clear grounding. Make that part of the room feel designed and used. Another interface pass would not close this largest visual gap.

## Hard visual bugs and visible limitations

- **A:** No unequivocal hard rendering failure or broken text layout is proved by these A stills. The clipped character in the wide view, flat-looking exit, and triangular hover effect are visible presentation/finish problems, not evidence of missing geometry or a runtime failure.
- **B:** Large areas are so dark that architectural surfaces and the shelf structures lose legibility. This is a severe visibility defect in the supplied images. The narrow mobile brand stack is cramped, and the result contains long red horizontal bars across the room. Their intended behavior cannot be inferred from stills, so they are not labeled collision or rendering bugs.

## Absolute decision

A is the clear A/B winner and a substantial developed scene, but I would not sign off that it has reached the supplied complete, developed reference standard across environment, character presentation, and mechanisms. B remains clearly below that standard. Relative improvement is not an absolute pass.
