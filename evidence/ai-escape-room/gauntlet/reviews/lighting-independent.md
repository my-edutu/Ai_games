## Locked X/Y judgment

X is more professionally finished because its room holds up across both the focal point and the perimeter. The central table, floor transitions, fireplace, cabinets and small shelf contents show more integrated contact and shadow relationships. Wood, brass, glass, bone, painted surfaces and framed artwork carry visibly different responses to the light. Layered trim, joinery, overlapping objects, scale changes and less uniform prop placement produce a much more fully authored environment.

Y has a coherent palette, a readable central objective, plausible basic room proportions and useful warm light. Its large repeated wood/plaster surfaces, uniformly clean prop silhouettes, sparse symmetrical shelving and simpler surface/contact response leave a clear finish gap. The floating mascot may be an intentional design choice; its lack of floor contact is not treated as an error.

This decision does **not** reward darkness. X's brighter wall-sconce, portrait/fireplace and central-table regions preserve the same advantages in material response, geometry and object integration. Its dark regions sometimes obscure detail. Y's readability is useful, but visibility alone does not equal a completed environment.

A is the stronger direction for the lighting/material pass. Its furniture close-up is the clearest evidence: the desktop carries a controlled warm sheen, chair arms and seat retain depth, brass details separate from dark wood, and the area beneath the desk remains grounded. B makes the wood grain more visible but gives much of the room a similar broadly lit, pale wood treatment. The warm and cool ceiling spots in B draw attention without producing an equally persuasive local light story in the surrounding room.

A has limits: some left-wall and shelf details remain too subdued, green upholstery and cloth are still simple, and small props lack the fine contact and material variation seen in X. Darkness hiding unfinished areas receives no credit. Static images do not establish the implementation or physical correctness of any particular light.

## Biggest remaining visible gap

**The room needs a fuller authored environment, with more specific geometry, material diversity and integrated room dressing.** The A/B difference improves presentation, but both retain essentially the same sparse, clean, repeated asset vocabulary. X maintains meaningful variation from the floor through the shelves to the ceiling. A lighting adjustment alone will not close that difference.

The next actionable outcome is a complete representative room section at the target finish: detailed shelf construction; varied books and objects with believable scale, overlap and contact; wood grain aligned to construction and finishes that vary plausibly; distinct cloth, leather, glass, brass, painted metal and stone; and narrative props arranged as if somebody used the space. Carry that density and quality through the remaining walls, floor and furniture so the central desk is not the only resolved area. Preserve A's light shaping while making the darker surfaces readable enough to judge.

Re-review the same room and furniture views against X at comparable 3D viewport scale. Success should be visible in seated props, differentiated surfaces in both light and shade, less generic repetition, coherent room dressing and a consistent finish across the entire room. The objective is to meet or exceed the reference's room finish, not only to show a change from an earlier render.

The simple floating character is visible, but these stills cannot establish complete character animation, acting, interaction or gameplay. Those areas remain outside the scope of this lighting/material and room-completeness review.

## Blindness and comparison limits

Labels were concealed and no mapping, source, git state, prior reviews or runtime were inspected. Input inspection was restricted to the six assigned images. X/Y was locked before A/B was opened. However, branding visible within an image, recognizable art, unequal cropping, image format and resolution can reveal identity or influence judgment. This is a label-concealed independent visual judgment; it is not a guarantee of full identity blindness. No conclusion depends on inferred source identity or chronology.
