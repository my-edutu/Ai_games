# Art Direction

## Viewer promise

Viewers watch a small autonomous climber fight upward through a tower that feels impossibly larger than the character, while machinery, hazards and guardians turn each ascent into a visible risk/recovery story.

## Pillars

1. **Monumental scale** — converging tower planes, distant structures, atmospheric layers and foreground occlusion keep the climber visually small.
2. **Readable danger** — each hazard class has a different silhouette; telegraphs and guardians must read before effects peak.
3. **Physical ascent** — platforms have thickness, edge faces and mechanical lighting; the climber is articulated and movement-correlated.
4. **Sector identity** — foundry, ruins, storm, clockwork and void use distinct environmental palettes and ambient treatments.
5. **Broadcast clarity** — the world remains the hero. HUD is a restrained overlay and clean-feed produces a pure gameplay frame.

## Shape language

- Tower: heavy trapezoids, beams, deep faces and converging verticals.
- Climber: narrow human silhouette with readable limbs/head/back profile.
- Sentinel: compact armored wedge.
- Shooter: compact body with an unmistakable barrel profile.
- Guardian: broad multi-point silhouette, bright core and extended arms.
- Pickups: rotating diamond-like tokens, never generic circles.

## Palette roles

Neutral architecture stays dark. Theme accent colors illuminate edges and local mechanisms. White is reserved for high-priority telegraph/readability. Red is danger, cyan is climber/system energy, amber is milestone/reward. Meaning is reinforced by silhouette and motion rather than color alone.

## Forbidden shortcuts

No debug grid as the primary world. No rectangle/circle final actors. No permanent dashboard taking a large fraction of the viewport. No uncontrolled full-screen flashes. No effects that hide the climber, danger or route. No presentation change may fabricate authoritative outcomes.
