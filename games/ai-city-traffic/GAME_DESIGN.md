# Game design

## Loop

Seeded trips enter the road network, queue and cross protected junctions, arrive at their destination and free capacity for later trips. Citizens move across the same district on a separate connected walking network. Rising demand competes with safe throughput. The viewer can compare steady everyday traffic, rush-hour queues, a roadworks slowdown and recovery.

## Creative pillars

- **Every journey is real:** a visible vehicle has an origin and destination, and a completed trip advances measured throughput. Removing a stuck vehicle must never create a completion.
- **A readable living district:** city silhouettes, street markings and protected crossing phases explain movement at a glance.
- **Visible tradeoffs:** changing signal policy or demand produces genuine queue and travel-time changes, with no fabricated performance uplift.
- **Calm, detailed observation:** aerial views reveal flow; street and follow cameras show individual people and vehicle detail.

## Space and rules

The district's four north/south and four east/west road centerlines sit at -72, -24, 24 and 72 metres, with sixteen junctions and portals toward ±108 metres. Right-hand lanes connect through continuous turning paths. A reservation protects each junction; receiving space and crossing clearance are checked before admission.

Long queues can be rerouted through valid alternative paths while preserving the original destination. Entry metering limits overload; pending and rejected demand remain visible in authoritative metrics. Signal phases include north/south, east/west, pedestrian and clearance periods.

## Rhythm and observations

Three rule-driven patterns are everyday flow → rush-hour queues → metered recovery; balanced traffic → roadworks slowdown → alternate routing; and vehicle-green phases → protected pedestrian crossings → vehicle release. None forces a hidden win/loss. Completed trips, rolling flow and waiting vehicles explain progress in this endless simulation.
