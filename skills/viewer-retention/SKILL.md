# Viewer Retention

## Overview

Create renewable reasons to keep watching by making progress, stakes, uncertainty, agency, and resolution continuously legible. The core principle is **earn attention through meaningful state change, not hidden manipulation or constant stimulation**.

## Scope

Use for pacing, dramatic pattern diversity, interaction windows, records, stream entry points, intermissions, replays, recaps, and retention measurement. It does not authorize deceptive design, undisclosed odds changes, fabricated urgency, coercive spending, or guaranteed paid effects.

## Non-Negotiable Invariants

- A viewer entering at any time can understand the current objective and stakes quickly.
- Something meaningful changes within game-specific target bands, but quiet contrast remains intentional.
- Suspense comes from rules, uncertainty, agent decisions, and eligible recorded events.
- The engagement director cannot secretly rescue, kill, choose winners, falsify probabilities, or rewrite records.
- Audience interaction is optional, bounded, disclosed, and never required to prevent boredom caused by a weak core loop.
- Repetition is measured by dramatic patterns, content features, strategies, and presentation—not only different seeds.
- Wins, losses, near-misses, recovery, and restart all provide closure and a next question.
- Metrics are interpreted with guardrails for accessibility, fairness, policy, safety, and long-term trust.

## Workflow

### 1. Define the entry promise

For a viewer arriving mid-run, specify what they see in ten seconds, what question remains unresolved, and the next predictable payoff window. Use goal/progress/danger/record, not a wall of lore.

### 2. Map attention horizons

Design renewable questions across:

- 5–15 seconds: immediate movement, hazard, choice, or feedback;
- 30–120 seconds: tactical plan, vote, encounter, obstacle, or mini-milestone;
- 3–15 minutes: checkpoint, boss, wave, floor band, tournament round, crisis, or major decision;
- run: win/loss/record and narrative shape;
- multi-run: streak, season, championship, era, rivalry, or record chase.

Exact bands vary by game, but every horizon needs progress and closure.

### 3. Catalogue meaningful events

Define events that change at least one of:

- progress;
- danger;
- strategy;
- resource position;
- relationship/attachment;
- record probability;
- audience opportunity;
- visible world state.

Cosmetic variation alone does not reset the meaningful-event clock.

### 4. Design dramatic pattern diversity

Identify at least three desirable run shapes and failure patterns. Track sequences such as lead changes, setbacks, recoveries, bottlenecks, risk choices, milestones, and terminal causes. Add mechanics/content that create missing patterns rather than forcing them through hidden director intervention.

### 5. Build a bounded event director

The director observes normalized signals: time since meaningful event, danger, progress, novelty, repetition, run age, audience activity, visual density, and recovery risk.

It may choose only from game-authored eligible events with:

- declared effect bounds;
- cooldown and conflict group;
- per-run and intensity caps;
- named random stream;
- authoritative event representation;
- accessibility and presentation variants;
- no forced outcome flag.

Use hysteresis and quiet periods. Do not trigger something merely because a metric dipped momentarily.

### 6. Place interaction windows

Open interactions near comprehensible choices: route, modifier, resource trade-off, environmental event, cosmetic theme, or difficulty opportunity. Avoid interrupting decisive action or stacking votes so viewers cannot see consequences.

Every window includes anticipation, choice, countdown, result, application/queue state, consequence, and cooldown.

### 7. Design closure and continuation

At result:

- show cause and decisive moment;
- compare progress/record;
- acknowledge audience contribution proportionately;
- present a short replay/summary;
- preview the next seed/theme/challenge;
- restart within a declared band.

For long persistent worlds, create chapters, eras, crises, successions, championships, or territory milestones so viewers receive closure before the world ends.

### 8. Measure ethically

Track:

- ten-second comprehension success;
- time to first meaningful event for entrants;
- meaningful-event intervals;
- milestone/record/interaction cadence;
- run-duration and dramatic-pattern distribution;
- repeated content/strategy rate;
- viewer return and session duration by non-sensitive aggregate cohorts;
- vote participation and consequence visibility;
- abandonment around confusion, overload, long quiet periods, intermission, or repeated failure;
- accessibility and moderation impact;
- paid/free influence outcomes without optimizing guaranteed results.

Use experiments with predeclared hypotheses, guardrails, sample size, stop criteria, and rollback.

## Required Outputs

- entry-viewer comprehension specification;
- attention-horizon map;
- meaningful-event catalogue and event clock definition;
- dramatic-pattern taxonomy and diversity targets;
- engagement-director signals, eligibility, cooldown, caps, and prohibited powers;
- interaction cadence and consequence-visibility rules;
- result/replay/intermission/restart flow;
- repetition/novelty measurement plan;
- retention metric definitions, guardrails, experiment plan, and rollback;
- anti-dark-pattern and monetization-safety statement.

## Review Gate

Pass only when:

- new viewers understand objective, progress, and danger within the target entry test;
- meaningful-event intervals and quiet periods meet declared distributions across seeds;
- at least three dramatic patterns occur at healthy rates;
- interaction windows are comprehensible and their consequences are visible before another competing window;
- director actions are eligible, recorded, replayable, bounded, and cannot force results;
- wins/losses/intermissions create closure and a clear next question;
- repetition metrics include content, strategy, event sequence, and audiovisual pattern;
- experiments have guardrails for fairness, accessibility, moderation, performance, and long-term trust;
- no metric goal requires coercive spending, fake urgency, or hidden probability manipulation.

## Stop-Ship Failures

- secret rescue/kill when engagement drops;
- constant crises, particles, and loudness;
- interaction every minute regardless of context;
- paid effect visually or mechanically guarantees an outcome;
- stream cannot be understood without watching from run start;
- only average watch time measured;
- one showcase seed or scripted near-miss;
- intermission long enough to lose the next-run question;
- frequent events with no meaningful consequence;
- optimization ignores accessibility, policy, or viewer trust.
